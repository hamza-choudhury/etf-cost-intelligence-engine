The cost spread of 4 bps between State Street and BlackRock looks small in percentage terms but compounds into roughly 40 per year on a 100,000 position, or about 800 over twenty years before compounding. The cheapest funds, State Street and Xtrackers at 3 bps, are not the largest. BlackRock dominates on scale at $154.5bn while charging 7 bps. That fragmentation is the central finding. Cost leadership and scale leadership sit in different providers, so a client choosing purely on price gives up liquidity and secondary market depth, and a client choosing purely on scale pays for it. The set splits 4 physical and 2 synthetic, yet the cheapest funds here are physical, so the assumption that synthetic always costs less does not hold across this group.
